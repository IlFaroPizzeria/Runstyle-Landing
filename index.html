The problem

Every run starts with the same doubt.

Long sleeves or short?
Wind vest or not?
Racing flats or daily trainers?
And how long will today's session actually take?

Most runners figure this out by instinct, checking a generic weather app that has no idea what clothes or shoes you own, or how your body reacts. RunStylist tells you what to wear, which shoes make sense for today's session, and how long the whole thing will take.

How it works

Three steps, before every run.

01

Photograph your gear

The AI recognizes brand, model, material, and sleeve length — from your Nike AeroSwift Tights to your Hoka trainers. You confirm, and it's saved to your digital wardrobe.

02

Describe your workout

Where you're running, what time, the type of session, and, if you want, its full structure: reps, paces, rest.

03

Get your full outfit

RunStylist cross-checks your wardrobe with the weather and the session — clothes, shoes, and duration — before you walk out the door.

FAQ

Before you ask.

Do I need a lot of technical gear for this to work?+

No. It works with whatever you already own, even 4 or 5 pieces. The more you add — including your shoes — the more precise the combinations get.

How does it recognize my clothes and shoes from a photo?+

It analyzes the image and detects brand, model, garment type, sleeve length, and likely material. You confirm or correct the details before it's saved.

How does the shoe recommendation work?+

It matches the shoes in your wardrobe against the type of session — plated racing shoes for hard intervals or races, versatile trainers for tempo work, and max-cushion daily trainers for easy or recovery runs.

How does it calculate my workout duration?+

It uses the structure you enter — reps, distances, rest, warm-up, and cool-down — along with a reference of your recent level, to estimate the real total time of the session.

Does it connect with Garmin, Strava, or Apple Health?+

It's on the roadmap. The beta focuses first on the wardrobe and the recommendation; integrations come in later phases.

Is this a training or health app?+

No. RunStylist doesn't plan workouts or give medical advice — it focuses on what to wear, which shoes to grab, and how long you'll be out there.

When will it be available?+

We're in the validation and beta-building phase. Whoever joins the waitlist will be among the first to try it.
